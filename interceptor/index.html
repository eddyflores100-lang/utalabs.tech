A valid card is not
a standing order.

Audit time is not runtime. A tool that was clean last Tuesday can be invoked with today's poisoned prompt. The Interceptor rides along every call, checks it against the capability graph and your org's policy, and answers in one word — ALLOW or BLOCK — with a signed reason. The rest of the stack vouches for the agent. The Interceptor judges the request.

RULE. 04

Eight allowed calls can be one attack

Per-call verdicts miss the plot: every step individually ALLOW, the chain is the exfiltration. Trajectory analysis (v5.4, public roadmap) scores the session, not the sentence.

What ships today: 5 runtime policy rules, live since v5.0 — including the two signature blocks (.env reads, rm -rf), capability checks against the card, and the JSONL audit trail. The Interceptor has been enforcing in production since August 2026.
What's honest roadmap: the full capability graph per tool, org-level policy DSL (“score ≥ 8 AND no filesystem AND no shell”), agent+task identity on every execution, and trajectory scoring. Each dated on the public roadmap — not sold as done.
